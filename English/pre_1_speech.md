
Today, I’d like to introduce the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center, also known as JSLC. 

It is China’s oldest and most successful spaceport, and uniquely, it remains the only launch site in China that has sent Chinese astronauts into space. 

Located deep in the Gobi Desert of northwestern China, JSLC covers a vast area of 2,800 square kilometers. 

Thanks to its dry desert climate, it enjoys roughly 300 favorable launch days every year.

So, why is JSLC called China's oldest launch site? 

Its story began in 1958 when it was initially established as a missile test range. Affectionately known as 'Dongfeng Space City,' this site has witnessed numerous national breakthroughs. In 1960, China successfully launched its first domestically built missile, the Dongfeng-1, right here. During the 1960s, it hosted historic nuclear and missile-nuclear tests. Then, in April 1970, JSLC achieved a monumental milestone by launching China's very first artificial satellite, Dongfanghong-1, ushering the nation into the space age. A decade later, in 1980, it completed the successful flight test of China's first carrier rocket.

Beyond its rich history, JSLC stands out as China’s most successful spaceport. It is the birthplace of China’s crewed space exploration and space station programs. In November 1999, the experimental spacecraft Shenzhou-1 took off from here. Four years later, in October 2003, Shenzhou-5 carried China's first astronaut into orbit. By 2008, Shenzhou-7 achieved the nation's first spacewalk. Furthermore, JSLC laid the foundation for China's permanent space station by launching Tiangong-1 in 2011 and Tiangong-2 in 2016. In addition to human spaceflight, it is a premier hub for scientific research: launching commercial flights since 1987, the DAMPE dark matter satellite in 2015, and the Micius quantum satellite in 2016. It successfully reached its 100th orbital launch in 2018 and its 200th in 2023.

Looking at more recent data, JSLC maintains an impressive overall success rate of 96.15%, accounting for 10.46% of global launches in 2025. It has sent 30 astronaut person-times into space involving 25 different individuals. Notably, from Shenzhou-1 to Shenzhou-23, crewed launch success stands at a remarkable 100%. It has also hosted inspiring public science events like the 'Lecture from Space' sessions originating from the Tiangong space station.

> 发射成功率96.15%，2025年占全球发射10.46%；共送30人次（25名不同航天员）进入太空，神舟一号至二十三号载人发射成功率100%；并开展了“天宫课堂”科普活动。

Finally, the story of JSLC is also one of human perseverance. What started as a harsh, remote outpost nearly 70 years ago has been successfully transformed into a modern green space oasis through continuous development. Today, it functions not only as a vital scientific complex but also as an inspiring tourist destination. Since 1997, it has been open to domestic visitors under controlled conditions, allowing approved guests to tour designated areas like the Feitian Park free of charge and experience China's aerospace history firsthand.
